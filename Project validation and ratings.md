# Green-lighting a data-centric pipeline-failure forecaster

**Bottom line: build it.** This thesis — forecasting the *next run* of a real ETL/ingestion pipeline (Open-Meteo air-quality) from *data-centric* leading indicators (drift/PSI, freshness, completeness, API latency trend, sensor stability, threshold-proximity, rolling warning history), with an explicit failure taxonomy — occupies a genuine, still-open gap in the 2024–2026 literature. No peer-reviewed work was found that forecasts a data/ETL pipeline run's failure from data-quality signals with honest leakage-free evaluation; the nearest published areas are CI/CD *build*-failure prediction (a different pipeline type), drift *detection* (not run-failure forecasting), and vendor "predictive" marketing with no measured recall or lead time ([arXiv 2510.24142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24142); [arXiv 2406.11213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11213); [Acceldata](https://www.acceldata.io/blog/predictive-data-quality-stopping-failures-before-they-happen)). The methodology — XGBoost-primary on ~3k imbalanced rows, PR-AUC/recall, within-fold SMOTE, PSI-as-feature, TreeSHAP, calibration, infra-vs-data ablation, leaky-vs-clean comparison — is mainstream-correct and literature-backed for this regime ([Grinsztajn et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815); [scikit-learn calibration](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html)). The novelty lives in the *conjunction*, not in any single element, and three wording guardrails plus a handful of experimental additions separate a defensible thesis from one a committee can rebut. Overall quality: **a solid, honestly-scoped, build-ready contribution** — not world-first on any one axis, but a real and well-positioned intersection.

## Validating the five novelty claims against 2024–2026 evidence

**Claim 1 — per-failure-type predictability map (the HEADLINE): HOLDS.** This is the strongest-supported novelty. The leading 2024 AIOps survey never partitions failures into predictable vs unpredictable classes and reports neither recall nor lead time by failure type, stating only generally that "numerous failures evade advance notice" ([arXiv 2406.11213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11213)). A 2025 ML-observability study explicitly lists per-failure-type detection as unresolved — which information combinations detect failures such as data corruption vs policy misconfiguration "remains unknown" ([arXiv 2510.24142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24142)). Reporting a map of recall + lead-time *per class*, and naming externally-driven classes as not predictable, as a positive finding rather than a limitation, appears genuinely under-occupied. **Wording change:** phrase it as "to our knowledge, per-failure-type predictability is not reported explicitly in the surveyed AIOps/observability literature," never "no one has studied failure predictability" — the claim rests on the survey's silence, which is absence-of-evidence.

**Claim 2 — data-centric prediction vs infra signals: HOLDS WITH CAVEAT.** No peer-reviewed paper was found using drift/freshness/completeness as *features* to forecast a subsequent ingestion run; where a predictive model appears (the DZone Airflow write-up), it uses run-history features and data *volume* but explicitly omits freshness and drift, states no one-run-ahead horizon, and reports no recall/precision ([DZone](https://dzone.com/articles/airflow-dag-failure-detection-ai)). Two caveats are load-bearing. First, the **"% of values near a hazard threshold" signal is effectively absent from observability catalogs** — a current catalog lists ten signals (freshness lag, volume variance, schema drift, null spikes, distribution shifts, lineage breaks, test-failure rates, replication lag, query-latency spikes, consumer error rates) and neither threshold-proximity nor PSI appears by name ([Lumenalta](https://lumenalta.com/insights/10-data-observability-signals-that-prevent-pipeline-failures)) — but threshold *monitoring* is standard, so present it as "a threshold-proximity feature used predictively," not "a signal nobody has thought of." Second, the biggest risk here is **terminology collision**: published "CI/CD pipeline failure forecasting" is real but operates on software-*build* pipelines (GitHub Actions runs, commit churn, developer logins, repo metadata), not data-quality signals or ETL ([Mendeley dataset](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/mggwn7rj9f); [arXiv 2402.09651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09651)). **Flag prominently: a reviewer will conflate the thesis with the published CI/CD-build area unless the ETL-vs-CI/CD distinction is foregrounded in the first page of framing.**

**Claim 3 — forecasting the next run, not detection: HOLDS WITH CAVEAT.** The dominant paradigm is detection/alerting after a deviation has begun; a 2025 observability study found proactive/forecasting methods appear only as *prior research*, with none of the studied teams running them in production and drift treated as "a symptom to interpret, not a predictor" ([arXiv 2510.24142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24142)), and an Oct-2025 self-healing-pipeline preprint lists "forecast drift likelihood in advance" only as a *future* direction ([Preprints.org 202510.2522](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202510.2522/v1)). **Wording change (critical): do not write "vendors/tools only detect."** Vendors now use "predictive" language; Acceldata markets predictive data quality with illustrative lead-time anecdotes (30 min, 24 h) and risk scores but no measured accuracy/recall/lead-time and advises shadow-mode validation ([Acceldata](https://www.acceldata.io/blog/predictive-data-quality-stopping-failures-before-they-happen)). The defensible phrasing is: "as of 2026, no vendor publishes *measured* one-run-ahead failure-forecasting performance (recall + lead time); their validated capability is anomaly detection." Monte Carlo's current first-party predictive page could not be fetched (allowlist-blocked), so **if the thesis names Monte Carlo as detection-only, verify its live docs directly first** — this is an unverified item.

**Claim 4 — quantified lead-time metric: HOLDS.** Well-motivated: the AIOps survey confirms the field commonly reports lead time *without numbers* and treats missed detections "in general terms" ([arXiv 2406.11213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11213)). The honest caveat is that **"~1 run ahead" is a modest horizon** — frame it as the natural, most leakage-safe and verifiable horizon of next-run forecasting, not as a deep-lead-time achievement. Anchoring the metric to established prognostics vocabulary (Prognostic Horizon, warning time) strengthens it against the "is one step useful?" question.

**Claim 5 — leakage-gap demonstration: SOFTEN.** The idea of building a leaky model beside a clean one and reporting the gap as a contribution already has recent precedent: a Dec-2025 paper deliberately builds a leaky LSTM pipeline, lists a "systematic comparison" of clean vs leaky among its contributions, and quantifies the effect as RMSE gain up to ~20.5% under 10-fold CV ([arXiv 2512.06932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.06932)). That precedent is univariate temperature forecasting on ~1,462 records and says nothing about failure prediction, so the thesis's *domain-specific* demonstration remains distinct. **Wording change:** reframe from "novel idea" to "applying a known pitfall-demonstration method to the data-pipeline failure-forecasting sub-area, where same-run features are an especially tempting trap." The ~99% leaky-model figure is a credible illustration, consistent with documented same-run/target-leakage pitfalls ([Hex](https://hex.tech/blog/feature-leakage/); [R-bloggers Jan 2026](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2026/01/data-leakage-in-r-why-correct-evaluation-matters-even-when-metrics-do-not-change/)).

**Supporting — cross-dataset (two cities, same API): keep "mild."** Two sites sharing one Open-Meteo schema hold feature semantics fixed and vary only the source, a far easier transfer than the cross-schema/cross-system challenge the field treats as hard. Call it "cross-source generalization under a shared schema (a mild OOD check)" everywhere and report per-site results; never let it drift toward "strong domain transfer."

## Validating the methodology: every major choice confirmed

**Tree models over deep learning — strongly defensible.** The foundational evidence still holds: Grinsztajn et al. show tree-based models (XGBoost, RF, GBTs) remain state-of-the-art on medium tabular data, because NNs are biased toward overly-smooth solutions, are not robust to uninformative features, and lose information in the original feature basis — and tuning does not close the gap ([arXiv 2207.08815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815)). Their benchmark used datasets filtered to **≥3,000 samples**, so trees' advantage is strongest exactly in this thesis's ~3k regime. Shwartz-Ziv & Armon independently found XGBoost outperforms deep tabular models including TabNet and that deep winners failed to generalize — directly undercutting any TabNet-as-primary argument ([ar5iv 2106.03253](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.03253)). **The one current competitor a committee will raise is not LSTM/Transformer/TabNet but TabPFN v2** (Hollmann et al., *Nature* 637:319–326, 2025), which beats trees only "with up to 10,000 samples" and carries practical caps ([ar5iv 2505.20003](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2505.20003)); a Dec-2025 benchmark review finds tabular foundation models win ~83% overall but with a regime rule — **smaller datasets with many categorical features favor boosted trees** — and advises TFMs "only for very small datasets" ([Mindful Modeler](https://mindfulmodeler.substack.com/p/when-trees-still-beat-tabular-foundation)). Pre-empt TabPFN explicitly as out-of-scope (black-box in-context model, less amenable to the SHAP+ablation+calibration workflow, no established edge on imbalanced run-level logs), do not ignore it.

**PR-AUC + recall over accuracy — textbook-correct** for ~11% imbalance, where a majority predictor scores high accuracy while catching zero positives and ROC-AUC is over-optimistic because large true-negative counts suppress FPR ([Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/precision-recall-curve-is-more-informative-than-roc-in-imbalanced-data-4c95250242f6)). The committee-proofing step: report AUPRC **against the ~0.11 prevalence baseline** (not as an absolute), add F1 and/or MCC ([KDnuggets](https://www.kdnuggets.com/why-most-people-misuse-smote-and-how-to-do-it-right)), and state the operating threshold for any recall figure.

**SMOTE within-fold — correct, with one weakness to defend.** Resampling must run inside each CV fold on training data only; pre-split SMOTE lets synthetic points land in both train and test and inflates metrics ([KDnuggets](https://www.kdnuggets.com/why-most-people-misuse-smote-and-how-to-do-it-right)). The thesis does this right. **The attackable weakness is doubling SMOTE *and* class weights** — both address imbalance, and stacking them can over-correct and hurt precision/calibration. **Defense:** treat it as an ablation (SMOTE-only, weights-only, both, none) and let PR-AUC choose; class weights alone often match or beat SMOTE on tabular data. With only ~330 minority rows, SMOTE's interpolation also has few real points to work from — flag elevated boundary-noise risk.

**PSI as a predictor feature — legitimate but probed.** PSI is a valid industry-standard drift metric with conventional thresholds (<0.1 none, 0.1–0.25 moderate, ≥0.25 significant) ([Arthur AI](https://docs.arthur.ai/docs/population-stability-index-psi-metrics)). Repurposing it as an *engineered predictor* is reasonable and mildly novel — **but no peer-reviewed source endorses PSI-as-feature, so present it as a modest contribution, not established practice.** Three committee attacks and defenses: **binning sensitivity** (fix the scheme up front, show PSI at 10 vs 20 bins); **reference-window stability** (define and justify a known-good baseline, keep it fixed); and **leakage** (PSI for run *t* must use only data up to *t* — the leaky-vs-clean comparison must explicitly cover the PSI feature's temporal construction).

**TreeSHAP — the de facto 2025–2026 standard**, including in regulated finance ([TechBullion](https://techbullion.com/shap-values-in-u-s-financial-machine-learning-from-technique-to-supervisory-default/); [MetricGate Jan 2026](https://metricgate.com/docs/shap-values/)). The current caveat to acknowledge: a 2026 paper shows gradient boosting's sequential residual fitting concentrates importance on whichever correlated feature is selected first ("first-mover bias"), so TreeSHAP faithfully propagates that bias and rankings shift across seeds ([arXiv 2603.22346](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22346)). **Defense:** report feature correlations, check attribution stability across seeds, and lean on the *ablation* — not SHAP — for the causal "data signals add value" claim.

**Probability calibration — appropriate and necessary** for XGBoost/RF, whose raw scores are not well-calibrated. With ~3k rows and ~330 positives, **isotonic risks overfitting, so Platt/sigmoid is the safer default** ([scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html)). The critical interaction: SMOTE distorts the base rate, so **calibration must be fit on un-resampled, held-out data** within the same time-based discipline — a committee will specifically probe whether calibration was done on resampled data. Report reliability diagrams and Brier/ECE before vs after.

**Infra-vs-data ablation — the rigorous way to prove the value-add**, stronger than SHAP alone. Leave-out/group ablation (retrain without a feature group, measure the drop) is the principled measure of a group's contribution and answers "does this improve performance," whereas SHAP answers only "what did the model use" ([arXiv 1910.00174](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00174); [arXiv 2605.22462](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.22462)). Strengthen it by reporting PR-AUC with confidence intervals (so "combined > infra-only" is shown real on ~3k rows), retraining and re-tuning each ablated model rather than masking at inference, and keeping the same time-based folds across all three arms.

## Validating objectives and the research gap

The **eight objectives are complete, measurable, and aligned to the gap**: logging data-centric signals, leakage-free next-run prediction, proving data beats infra (ablation), mapping predictability per failure type (headline), quantifying lead time, demonstrating the leakage gap (headline), cross-dataset validation (supporting), and an explainable date-input interface. Each maps to a specific measurable output — recall, lead-time distribution, per-class metrics, ablation deltas with CIs, leaky-vs-clean gap — which is exactly what a committee wants. Two honest deductions: the explainability objective is **practical delivery, not a research contribution**, and should be framed as such; and the lead-time objective is modest at one step. Nothing material is missing, though adding F1/MCC and explicit confidence-interval reporting to the stated deliverables would tighten measurability.

**The gap is real and still open in 2026.** Across peer-reviewed work ([arXiv 2406.11213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11213); [arXiv 2510.24142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24142)), preprints ([Preprints.org 202510.2522](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202510.2522/v1)), and vendor material ([Acceldata](https://www.acceldata.io/blog/predictive-data-quality-stopping-failures-before-they-happen); [Databricks Lakehouse Monitoring](https://docs.databricks.com/sap/lakehouse-monitoring); [Bigeye](https://docs.bigeye.com/docs)), the data-signal-driven, leakage-free, one-run-ahead *forecast* of an ETL run — scored by lead time, reported per failure type — is not jointly demonstrated. The honest qualifier the thesis must preserve: much of this rests on **absence-of-evidence** (paywalled IEEE/ACM full texts were not exhaustively mined; a niche 2025–2026 workshop paper could exist), so committee-facing text should hedge with "to our knowledge / in the surveyed literature," and the gap's force comes from the *conjunction*, since each element exists somewhere individually.

## Final recommendations before building — prioritized

1. **Foreground the ETL-vs-CI/CD distinction on page one.** This is the single highest-leverage fix. Published "pipeline failure forecasting" means software *builds*; state explicitly and early that this thesis forecasts *data/ETL ingestion* runs from *data-quality* signals, or a reviewer will dismiss it as redone CI/CD work ([arXiv 2402.09651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09651); [Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/mggwn7rj9f)).
2. **Soften two phrasings now.** Replace "vendors/tools only detect" with "no *measured* one-run-ahead forecasting is published (vendors use predictive language)," and reframe the leakage demo as a domain-specific application of a known pitfall-demo, not a new concept ([Acceldata](https://www.acceldata.io/blog/predictive-data-quality-stopping-failures-before-they-happen); [arXiv 2512.06932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.06932)).
3. **Verify Monte Carlo's live predictive claims directly** before naming it detection-only; its first-party page was allowlist-blocked in this research and remains unverified ([Sifflet comparison](https://www.siffletdata.com/blog/monte-carlo-vs-acceldata)).
4. **Make the per-type predictability map the titled headline contribution** — report PR-AUC/recall/lead-time per taxonomy class and explain unpredictable classes (e.g., provider-outage API_SLA_BREACH) via random-vs-systematic failure theory. This is the strongest novelty; elevate it from a table row ([arXiv 2510.24142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24142)).
5. **Demonstrate the leakage effect on your own data** — report metrics under a naive/same-run split next to the clean t+1 split, converting the leakage claim from assertion to evidence ([arXiv 2512.06932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.06932)).
6. **Guarantee statistical power: ensure ≥30 test failures** and report every metric with bootstrap/repeated-CV confidence intervals; ~330 positives gives wide intervals, so point estimates alone will be attacked.
7. **Add the baselines that make the "data beats infra" claim fair:** an infra-only arm, a rule-based threshold baseline, and a Dummy/majority baseline, all retrained and re-tuned on identical time-based folds, with the ablation gap reported on PR-AUC with CIs ([arXiv 1910.00174](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00174)).
8. **Fix the SMOTE handling:** run SMOTE-only / weights-only / both / none as an ablation, and calibrate on *un-resampled* held-out data ([KDnuggets](https://www.kdnuggets.com/why-most-people-misuse-smote-and-how-to-do-it-right); [scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html)).
9. **Lock the PSI construction:** fixed, documented binning with a 10-vs-20-bin sensitivity check, a justified fixed reference window, and strictly causal computation ([Arthur AI](https://docs.arthur.ai/docs/population-stability-index-psi-metrics)).
10. **Pre-empt TabPFN v2** in the model-choice section as the one current credible alternative, scoped out for good reasons ([ar5iv 2505.20003](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2505.20003); [Mindful Modeler](https://mindfulmodeler.substack.com/p/when-trees-still-beat-tabular-foundation)).

## Honest risks and what a tough examiner will push on

**Small dataset (~3k rows, ~330 positives).** Trees are strongest here, but power is limited and SMOTE quality suffers. *Pre-empt:* CIs on all metrics, simple models, honest scope, ≥30 test failures.

**Mild cross-validation.** Two cities on one API is near-duplicate transfer. *Pre-empt:* label it "mild OOD check," report per-site, flag genuine multi-API validation as future work — never claim strong transfer ([arXiv 2412.15445](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15445v2)).

**Externally-driven unpredictable failures.** A provider-outage API_SLA_BREACH leaves no leading signal in the pipeline's own logs. *Pre-empt:* turn this into the per-type finding, grounded in random-vs-systematic failure theory, not a hidden weakness.

**Narrow domain.** One public air-quality API limits external validity. *Pre-empt:* argue the *method* (data-centric indicators, t+1 label, lead-time scoring, per-type map) is domain-agnostic and position broader validation as future work.

**Absence-of-evidence claims.** Several gap statements rest on not finding a matching paper and on allowlist-blocked or paywalled sources. *Pre-empt:* bounded wording throughout ("to our knowledge," "in the surveyed literature"), keep peer-reviewed sources separate from vendor/preprint, and preserve the unverified flags (Monte Carlo live page, PSI-as-feature lacking peer-reviewed endorsement, the scikit-learn stable page corroborated via archived mirrors).

**Modest one-step horizon.** A committee will ask whether one run of warning is useful. *Pre-empt:* justify it against the pipeline's remediation window, report the lead-time distribution, note t+1 is the most leakage-safe horizon, and cite multi-step as future work.
