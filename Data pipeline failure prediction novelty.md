# Novelty / Contributions

> Drafted for a master's thesis committee. The central move is defended as the *conjunction* of four choices on a real ingestion pipeline, not the invention of any one choice. Every claim is graded STRONG / MODERATE / SUPPORTING against the 2024–2026 literature, and the choices that are merely standard good practice are listed plainly so the committee sees the student is not over-claiming.

The thesis builds an early-warning system that predicts data-pipeline failures **one run ahead** from **data-centric leading indicators** (data drift / PSI, freshness lag, completeness, API response-time trend, sensor stability, percentage of values near a hazard threshold, rolling warning history) engineered from a pipeline's own run logs, validated on a real Open-Meteo air-quality ingestion pipeline with an explicit failure-rule taxonomy. The literature review (47 papers; the three research-note files behind this section) shows that each of these elements exists *somewhere*, but the **intersection** — data-signal-driven, leakage-free, one-run-ahead *forecasting* of a real ETL/ingestion run, scored by lead time and reported per failure type with a cross-source generalization check — is not jointly demonstrated by any single surveyed work. That intersection, not any individual technique, is the contribution.

---

## 1. Novelty statement

**This thesis is the first to forecast data-pipeline-run failures one run ahead using data-centric leading indicators engineered from the pipeline's own logs — rather than infrastructure telemetry or post-hoc drift detection — and to validate that forecast on a real public ingestion pipeline with an explicit failure taxonomy, a quantified lead-time metric, per-failure-type predictability analysis, and a cross-source generalization test.**

---

## 2. Novelty claims (ranked strongest-first)

### Claim 1 — Data-centric signals used as *predictors* of a future run, not as the thing being monitored — STRONG
**Closes gaps (2) and (3).** Across both the observability industry and the academic drift literature, data-centric signals (drift, freshness, schema, nulls, distribution) are consistently treated as **the object being monitored / the symptom to detect**, never as leading-indicator features fed to a model that forecasts a *subsequent run's* failure. Databricks explicitly frames data quality as a monitored object — tooling "validate[s] content against rules" and tracks "data quality metrics" — distinct from observability that "detects unknown issues" ([Databricks glossary](https://www.databricks.com/glossary/data-observability)). The D3Bench benchmark of Evidently AI, NannyML and Alibi-Detect on two real smart-building datasets frames drift strictly as **detection of change that has already occurred** and relates it to *model accuracy decline*, not pipeline-run failure ([arXiv:2404.18673v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18673v2)). Where a predictive model does appear, data signals are quarantined out of it: the DZone Airflow predictor uses data **volume** as its only data-centric input, while null ratios and distributions sit in a *separate* reactive anomaly-detection layer, not as predictor inputs ([DZone](https://dzone.com/articles/airflow-dag-failure-detection-ai)). The air-quality framing sharpens this: the **"% of values near a hazard threshold"** signal has **no analog** in any surveyed observability signal catalog (which stop at distribution shift / null spikes), suggesting genuine novelty in the feature construction itself, not just the framing ([Lumenalta](https://lumenalta.com/insights/10-data-observability-signals-that-prevent-pipeline-failures)). *Graded STRONG because the detect-vs-predict and monitored-object-vs-feature distinctions are explicit and repeated across the strongest sources, and no peer-reviewed study was found that uses drift/freshness as features to predict a subsequent ingestion run's failure.*

### Claim 2 — A data-pipeline-run *forecast*, not detection of an in-progress deviation or a retraining trigger — STRONG
**Closes gaps (3) and (1).** The dominant paradigm is **detection + alerting after a deviation has begun**, with "predictive"/"proactive" vendor language resolving on inspection into either early *detection* of a deviation already underway or unquantified, illustrative claims. Databricks' "proactive" observability "detect[s], diagnose[s], and prevent[s]" but **does not describe forecasting future pipeline failures** ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/glossary/data-observability)). Acceldata markets "predictive data quality… stopping failures before they happen," but its two lead-time figures ("30 min before a freshness SLA violation," "24 h before a downstream ML failure") are explicitly **hypothetical scenarios, not measured outcomes**, and it forecasts *data properties* (volume, freshness, nulls) rather than a modeled probability that the next run fails ([Acceldata](https://www.acceldata.io/blog/predictive-data-quality-stopping-failures-before-they-happen)). In the drift literature the signal is a **retraining trigger**, not a failure forecast: the ICSE 2025 DeepTest paper uses unsupervised drift detectors to decide *when to retrain* failure-prediction models, with no lead-time metric ([ICSE 2025 / DeepTest](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2025/deeptest-2025-papers/1/Improving-the-Reliability-of-Failure-Prediction-Models-through-Concept-Drift-Monitori)), and a 2025 unified survey frames the entire field around *detecting drift and adapting/retraining*, not forecasting a downstream failure event ([arXiv:2505.17902](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.17902)). *Graded STRONG: the predict-vs-trigger and forecast-vs-detect boundaries are clearly drawn in the strongest sources, and the one peer-review-adjacent article closest in intent (IJISAE 2026, below) discloses no horizon, data, or numbers.*

### Claim 3 — Per-failure-type predictability reported as a first-class *finding* — STRONG (as an empirical contribution)
**Closes gap (2) and adds a result of its own.** Analyzing failures by type and reporting that **some classes are inherently hard to predict from history** is the single most defensible candidate for genuine novelty. Mainstream predictive papers do *binary* prediction and do not decompose predictability by cause: MING notes failures arise from "a variety of reasons" but gives **no per-type predictability breakdown** ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/predicting-node-failure-in-cloud-service-systems/)), and the AIOps model-update study predicts a single binary outcome per dataset ([arXiv:2311.03213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03213)). Where predictability-by-characteristic appears, it is framed as a *limitation*, not a headline: the 2014 cloud-job study found per-user history essential and noted "failures that occur soon after a fault manifests are hard to predict early enough to act on" ([UBC PDF](https://blogs.ubc.ca/karthik/files/2014/09/rsda14.pdf)). The idea has a firm conceptual grounding in the reliability-engineering distinction between **random vs. systematic failures** ([IADC Lexicon](https://iadclexicon.org/glossary/global-standards/page/79/)), which lets the thesis explain *why* some taxonomy classes are unpredictable rather than merely observing it. *Graded STRONG as an empirical contribution, with the bounded wording the notes recommend: "to our knowledge, per-failure-type predictability is seldom reported explicitly in data-pipeline failure prediction; we make it a first-class finding" — not "no one has studied failure predictability."*

### Claim 4 — Empirical validation on a real public ingestion pipeline with an explicit failure taxonomy — MODERATE
**Closes gap (1).** Recent self-healing / predictive-pipeline work is **mostly conceptual or validated on curated/injected data**, not live production data, and none pairs a data-centric forecast with a labeled failure taxonomy. Bhattarai's "Scaling Generative AI for Self-Healing DevOps Pipelines" is **conceptual/literature-synthesis** with borrowed metrics and no own experiments ([Preprints.org 202506.1436](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202506.1436/v1)). The NCI master's thesis (Nukalapati, 2026) is empirical but on **60 injected failures in a controlled AWS CodeBuild setup**, uses build logs not data signals, and is reactive rather than predictive ([NCI thesis PDF](https://norma.ncirl.ie/9723/1/vinaynukalapati.pdf)). The closest-in-intent article, IJISAE 2026, claims "experimental evaluation" but **discloses no dataset, no real-vs-synthetic status, no numbers, and no horizon**, with a duplicated reference list flagged as a quality-control red flag and peer review unconfirmed for that item ([IJISAE 8381](https://ijisae.org/index.php/IJISAE/article/view/8381)). D3Bench *is* validated on two real datasets but benchmarks drift detection tools, not failure prediction ([arXiv:2404.18673v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18673v2)). *Graded MODERATE: real-data validation and an explicit taxonomy are each clearly uncommon and valued, but "real data" and "a taxonomy" are not themselves inventions — their force comes from being attached to the data-centric forecast in Claims 1–2.*

### Claim 5 — Quantified lead-time / early-warning horizon reported in a sub-area that usually omits it — MODERATE
**Closes gap (4).** Lead time is a mature, formally defined concept in **prognostics** (the "Prognostic Horizon," H = EOP − i, formalized by Saxena et al., IEEE Aerospace 2009) and in **online failure prediction** (data window, lead-time Δt_l, warning time; Salfner et al., ACM CSUR 2010), so the thesis **cannot claim to have invented it** ([NASA DASHlink PDF](https://c3.ndc.nasa.gov/dashlink/static/media/publication/2009_IEEEAerospace_MetricsEvaluation.pdf); [HPI lecture PDF](https://osm.hpi.de/depend/2012/11_proactive.pdf)). But mainstream cloud/pipeline ML papers frequently omit it: MING reports only that it predicts "before node failure actually happens," with no lead-time metric ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/predicting-node-failure-in-cloud-service-systems/)); the 2014 cloud-job paper predicts "at the quarter and half points" of runtime but reports no absolute lead times ([UBC PDF](https://blogs.ubc.ca/karthik/files/2014/09/rsda14.pdf)); the AIOps model-update study defines prediction *windows* and reports AUC, with **no lead-time metric** ([arXiv:2311.03213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03213)). *Graded MODERATE: defensible as a rigor / practical-usefulness contribution with the honest framing "we report a quantified early-warning horizon, often omitted in this sub-area" — not "we introduce lead time."*

### Claim 6 — Leakage-free, strictly one-run-ahead (t+1) design in a sub-area that under-audits it — MODERATE
**Closes gap (3).** Temporal/target leakage is a **named, surveyed pitfall** — Kapoor & Narayanan catalog temporal leakage (type L3.1) and pre-split oversampling (L1.2), finding leakage "a pitfall in every single case" across 20 papers affecting **329 papers** ([ar5iv/arXiv:2207.07048](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.07048)) — and time-aware splitting is already the documented norm in AIOps data-splitting studies (TOSEM 2021, DOI 10.1145/3447876; [Polytechnique Montréal](https://publications.polymtl.ca/47999/)). So this is **rigor, not invention**. Its force is that leakage is *under-audited in the specific failure-prediction literature*, and at least one comparable paper appears to leak: the Google-cluster task-failure study lists **event type** among its input attributes while also stating "the event type is the targeted class" — the label is among the features — yet reports ~99.7–99.8% accuracy, a red flag consistent with leakage ([IIETA study](https://www.iieta.org/node/10971)). *Graded MODERATE as a validity/rigor contribution; it would rise toward STRONG if the thesis **demonstrates** the leakage effect by showing inflated numbers under a naive split, as the "Hidden Leaks in Time Series Forecasting" paper does ([arXiv:2512.06932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.06932)).*

### Claim 7 — Cross-source generalization under a shared schema (train on one city, test on a second) — SUPPORTING
**Closes gap (5), partially.** Cross-dataset / cross-environment evaluation is **rare in comparable AIOps failure work and repeatedly flagged as a gap**: the AIOps model-update study reports "None is described" for cross-dataset ([arXiv:2311.03213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03213)); the Google-cluster task study is single-dataset with a 70/30 split ([IIETA](https://www.iieta.org/node/10971)); the LLM-era AIOps survey notes most works have "not systematically assessed generalizability across systems" ([arXiv:2406.11213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11213)). But the thesis's two-city, same-API setup is a **weaker, more favorable form of transfer** than the cross-schema / cross-system transfer the field treats as the real challenge — CroSysLog shows cross-system difficulty is driven by differing log content/schema, with naive supervised transfer collapsing to 24.75%–72.30% F1 ([arXiv:2412.15445](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15445v2)). *Graded SUPPORTING: honestly described as "cross-source generalization under a shared schema, a mild out-of-distribution check," it adds credibility; presented as "strong domain transfer," it would be easy to rebut.*

## 3. What is NOT novel — avoiding over-claim

The committee should see these stated plainly as **standard good practice**, carrying no novelty weight on their own:

- **RF / XGBoost as the model family.** Standard failure-prediction choice ([arXiv:2311.03213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03213)); tree-based models on tabular/imbalanced data are routine comparison material ([Amrita](https://www.amrita.edu/publication/comprehensive-analysis-of-random-forest-and-xgboost-performance-with-smote-adasyn-and-gnus-upsampling-under-varying-imbalance-levels/)).
- **SMOTE + class weights for ~11% imbalance.** A thoroughly standard, commodity mitigation — only *new resampling/architectural methods* (e.g., SMOTified-GAN) are published as contributions ([arXiv:2108.03235](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03235)). SMOTE is widely noted as a default-but-not-magic baseline often beaten by plain class-weighting ([Analytics Vidhya](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/blog/2026/07/class-imbalance-ml/)). An ~11% minority class is also *milder* than typical cloud-failure settings (0.05–1.5%), so SMOTE+weights is merely adequate here, not a result. **Apply resampling within-fold / after the split**, since pre-split oversampling is itself the L1.2 leakage pitfall ([Kapoor & Narayanan](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.07048)).
- **PR-AUC / recall for imbalanced evaluation.** Recommended best practice, not a contribution ([MachineLearningMastery](https://machinelearningmastery.com/algorithm-showdown-logistic-regression-vs-random-forest-vs-xgboost-on-imbalanced-data/)).
- **Basic drift metrics (PSI, KS).** PSI is a standard distribution-shift magnitude metric with conventional thresholds (~0.1 moderate, ~0.25 significant) ([NannyML PSI](https://nannyml.com/blog/population-stability-index-psi)); using drift detectors is routine ([arXiv:2505.17902](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.17902)). Novelty is in *repurposing PSI as a predictor feature*, not in PSI itself.
- **Time-based splits / next-step targets as a mechanism.** The correct-but-known norm in this domain; AIOps work already predicts "next week from previous week" with chronological splits ([arXiv:2311.03213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03213)). The contribution is the *audit and the t+1 discipline in a sub-area that skips it*, not the splitting technique.
- **Explainability via feature importances.** A reasonable usefulness feature; the thesis should frame the explainable date-input interface as *practical delivery*, not a research contribution, since weak explainability is a field gap the thesis *addresses* rather than a method it *invents*.
- **Periodic retraining as live data grows.** Standard MLOps hygiene.

## 4. Positioning vs 2024–2026 work

Four adjacent families bracket the thesis. **Data-pipeline observability** (Databricks, Monte Carlo's five pillars, Lumenalta, Qualdo) monitors current data health and alerts on deviations already underway; its "predictive" language resolves into early detection, and self-healing is defined as reactive auto-remediation — detect → diagnose → correct ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/glossary/data-observability); [Qualdo](https://www.qualdo.ai/blog/why-self-healing-data-pipelines-replace-on-call-systems-in-2026/)). **Self-healing pipeline papers** that are genuinely engineered (NCI's six-agent LLM; Bhattarai's DevOps synthesis) are reactive, concentrated on CI/CD *build* errors, use code/build logs rather than data signals, and are conceptual or validated on injected failures ([NCI thesis](https://norma.ncirl.ie/9723/1/vinaynukalapati.pdf); [Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202506.1436/v1)). **Data-drift work** detects change and triggers retraining, relating drift to model-accuracy decline, not pipeline-run failure ([D3Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18673v2); [ICSE 2025](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2025/deeptest-2025-papers/1/Improving-the-Reliability-of-Failure-Prediction-Models-through-Concept-Drift-Monitori)). **AIOps / cloud failure prediction** *does* forecast, but from infrastructure/scheduling/hardware telemetry and logs (SMART attributes, CPU/memory/GPU, scheduling class, node error counters), rarely reports lead time, and is almost always single-dataset ([arXiv:2311.03213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03213); [arXiv:2402.00034](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.00034); [arXiv:2406.11213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11213)). The decisive framing: **forecasting exists for build pipelines, detection exists for data pipelines, but data-signal-driven forecasting of data-pipeline runs is the uncovered intersection.**

| Line of work (representative) | Data-centric predictors | One-run-ahead horizon / lead time | Explicit failure taxonomy | Real-data validation | Cross-dataset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Data observability** (Databricks, Lumenalta, Qualdo) | Signals *monitored*, not predictors | No (detects in-progress deviations) | No (informal categories) | n/a (platform) | n/a |
| **Self-healing** (NCI LLM; Bhattarai) | No (build logs / code) | No (reactive) | Build-error categories only | Injected/curated or synthesis | No |
| **Data-drift** (D3Bench; ICSE 2025 DeepTest) | Drift *detected*, not a predictor | No (retraining trigger) | No | Yes (D3Bench, 2 real datasets) | No |
| **AIOps / cloud failure pred.** (model-update study; UPL; MING) | No (infra/HW/scheduling, logs) | Rare (windows, not lead time) | No (binary outcome) | Yes (own system) | No (single-dataset) |
| **IJISAE 2026** (closest in intent) | Partial (obs. metrics) | Not stated | No | Not disclosed | No |
| **THIS THESIS** | **Yes** (drift/freshness/completeness/latency/near-threshold) | **Yes** (t+1, quantified lead time) | **Yes** (DATA_QUALITY_FAILURE, API_SLA_BREACH, …) | **Yes** (real Open-Meteo ingestion) | **Yes** (two-city, same schema — mild) |

## 5. How to strengthen the novelty further

1. **Demonstrate the leakage effect on your own data.** Report metrics under a naive (leaky, shuffled or same-run) split next to the leakage-free t+1 split, showing how much the naive numbers inflate — mirroring the "Hidden Leaks in Time Series Forecasting" demonstration of ~19–20% RMSE gains under improper CV ([arXiv:2512.06932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.06932)). This converts Claim 6 from an assertion into evidence and pre-empts the "you just followed known practice" rebuttal.
2. **Anchor the lead-time metric to the established prognostics vocabulary.** Define your horizon in the terms of the formal **Prognostic Horizon** and α-λ / warning-time family (Saxena et al. 2009) ([NASA DASHlink PDF](https://c3.ndc.nasa.gov/dashlink/static/media/publication/2009_IEEEAerospace_MetricsEvaluation.pdf)). Borrowing the mature definitions makes Claim 5 defensible and shows the committee the metric is grounded, not ad hoc.
3. **Make per-failure-type predictability an explicit, explained result.** Report PR-AUC/recall/lead time *per taxonomy class*, state which classes are predictable and which are not, and explain unpredictable classes via the **random vs. systematic failure** distinction ([IADC Lexicon](https://iadclexicon.org/glossary/global-standards/page/79/)). This elevates the strongest novelty candidate (Claim 3) from a table row to a titled contribution.

## 6. Honest caveats / threats to the novelty claim

- **"Cross-dataset" is a weak, same-API form of transfer.** Two cities on the same Open-Meteo schema hold feature semantics fixed and vary only the source — far easier than the cross-schema/cross-system transfer the field treats as hard ([arXiv:2412.15445](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15445v2)). *Pre-empt:* call it "cross-source generalization under a shared schema (a mild OOD check)" everywhere, and never label it strong domain transfer. Grade it SUPPORTING, as above.
- **Some failure types are externally driven and genuinely unpredictable from history.** An API_SLA_BREACH caused by a provider outage leaves no leading signal in the pipeline's own logs; the 2014 cloud-job study already flags failures "that occur soon after a fault manifests" as unpredictable in-horizon ([UBC PDF](https://blogs.ubc.ca/karthik/files/2014/09/rsda14.pdf)). *Pre-empt:* turn this into Claim 3 — report it as a finding grounded in random-vs-systematic failure theory, not as a weakness to hide.
- **Air-quality ingestion is a narrow domain.** A single public API in one domain limits external validity. *Pre-empt:* state scope honestly, argue the *method* (data-centric leading indicators, t+1 label, lead-time scoring, per-type analysis) is domain-agnostic, and position broader validation as future work — the same limitation the comparable NCI thesis declared about its 60 injected failures ([NCI thesis](https://norma.ncirl.ie/9723/1/vinaynukalapati.pdf)).
- **Individual techniques are all standard.** A tough examiner will note RF/XGBoost, SMOTE, PR-AUC, PSI, and time-based splits are each off-the-shelf. *Pre-empt:* concede this openly (Section 3) and keep the novelty located in the **conjunction** on a real ingestion pipeline — the explicit position of this thesis.
- **Absence-of-evidence is not proof.** Several gap claims rest on *not finding* a matching paper (no peer-reviewed data-signal-driven next-run ingestion predictor; no per-type predictability contribution; no cross-environment data-pipeline transfer). *Pre-empt:* use bounded wording ("to our knowledge," "seldom reported") rather than "no one has," and separate peer-reviewed sources from the vendor/marketing and unverified items below.
